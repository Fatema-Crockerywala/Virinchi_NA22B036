    Case notes

    Keep the desk. Put the handover on the slip.

    I would keep this product. Its unit-level records are sound. The weak point is the moment a unit changes hands away from the counter: the register keeps naming the wrong person, and the truth lives in a message the next shift has to find.

    Handled well

    The issue slip refuses a bad pickup

    While issuing the Film Society kit, PR-06 never appears because it is in repair. With AD-12 already out on RQ-214, I tried to add AD-15 and the desk refused: the approved quantity was already on loan. A digital camera was refused too, because the request does not include one.

    What I would change

    Saturday’s slip points at the wrong person

    After Friday’s partial return, Leela walks in with an adapter. Slip L-404 still says Kabir, shows no overdue state, and sits beside Tara’s microphone with the same one-click Record return button. Kabir’s message names Leela but not the unit, so I had to join the desk book to the register by hand and nothing asked me to check the tag.

    The change: custody travels with the unit

    1. Friday 3:45 pm. Choose Kabir. The counter shows why he may collect, the USB-C note, and one prefilled kit form.
    2. Friday 7:30 pm. Account for each unit. Marking the adapter Still out requires who has it and when it is promised.
    3. Saturday 9:00 am. Choose Leela. Her card names slip L-404, shows it overdue, and closes only when the tag in hand matches.

    What I would keep

    • One slip per physical unit
    • The approval and quantity checks
    • The desk book for anything a form cannot hold
    • Inventory and the register as the reference views

    What I left out

    • Barcode scanning, which needs hardware at the desk
    • Notifications to borrowers
    • Staff accounts and permissions
    One observation at a real counter

    Watch the first morning handover after a partial return. When the borrower’s friend arrives, does the incoming staff member reach the right slip and read the physical tag without opening the desk book or asking anyone?
